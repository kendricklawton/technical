# Domain 10: Behavioral & Career Leadership — Essential Frameworks & Executive Guide

> **Core Focus:** Staff+ Engineering Archetypes, Technical Strategy, RFC & Architecture Decision Record (ADR) Frameworks, Influence Without Authority, Conflict Resolution, Mentorship, and Executive Behavioral Interviewing.

---

## 1. Foundational Engineering Leadership Literature

### 1.1 [Staff Engineer: Leadership Beyond the Management Track (Will Larson)](https://staffeng.com/)
* **Canonical Scope:** The definitive guide to the individual contributor (IC) leadership career path.
* **The Four Staff Archetypes:**
  1. **The Tech Lead:** Guides execution for a particular team, partners with engineering manager, owns technical roadmap.
  2. **The Architect:** Responsible for the success, design, and direction of a large technical area or entire engineering organization.
  3. **The Solver:** Digs into deep, complex, ambiguous problems and solves them cleanly (troubleshooter/firefighter).
  4. **The Right Hand:** Partners directly with a senior executive (VP or CTO) to borrow authority and execute broad technical initiatives.

### 1.2 [The Staff Engineer's Path (Tanya Reilly)](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/the-staff-engineers/9781098118723/)
* **Canonical Scope:** Practical execution as a senior IC leader.
* **Key Lessons:**
  * **Navigating Ambiguity:** Knowing what to build when nobody gives you a ticket or specification.
  * **Being a Force Multiplier:** Elevating the entire engineering organization through tooling, architecture standards, and mentorship rather than just writing more individual code.
  * **Energy Management:** Avoiding burnout by focusing on highest-leverage organizational bottlenecks.

### 1.3 [Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) & RFC Process](https://adr.github.io/)
* **Canonical Scope:** Codifying institutional architectural memory.
* **ADR Structure:** Title, Status (Proposed, Accepted, Superseded), Context (What problem are we solving?), Decision (What are we doing?), Consequences (What positive and negative tradeoffs arise?).

## 2. Behavioral Interview Frameworks & Executive Presence

### 2.1 The Executive STAR Method
* **Situation:** Set the business context with real stakes (e.g. "\$20M ARR customer migration on the line, system down 4 hours/week").
* **Task:** Define the mandate and why *you* had to lead it.
* **Action:** Deep-dive into technical leadership: how you navigated conflicting opinions, created alignment, and influenced without authority.
* **Result:** Concrete, quantifiable business metrics (e.g., "Reduced MTTR by 72%, saved \$1.2M annually, zero Sev-1 incidents in 12 months").

### 2.2 Crucial Conversations & Radical Candor
* Giving candid, constructive feedback directly while showing deep personal care.
* Resolving cross-team friction between Product, Security, and Engineering through shared business goals.

## 3. Staff-Level Interview Angle & Leadership Reflexes

1. **"How do you handle pushback from senior engineers on your platform strategy?":**
   * *Staff Reflex:* Never use executive mandate. First, listen deeply to understand their valid operational concerns and failure modes. Invite them to co-author the RFC. Run a controlled proof-of-concept (POC) with their team. Demonstrate measurable improvements in their deployment speed and on-call toil.
2. **"Tell me about a major architectural failure you led":**
   * *Executive Signal:* Complete radical ownership (zero blaming subordinates or vendors), transparent post-mortem analysis, and systemic corrective actions that permanently eliminated that class of failure.
